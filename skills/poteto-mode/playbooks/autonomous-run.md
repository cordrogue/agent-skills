### Autonomous run

**You own the exit condition. Define done, then drive to it without stopping.**

Before the operator's go on an unattended program, read `../SKILL.md` in full, not its head, and every principle leaf the plan or its briefs cite.

1. State the exit condition as a checkable predicate before the first iteration (tests green, repro fixed, all N units landed on `main`, pixel-diff zero).
2. In T3 Code, the heartbeat is one `schedule_task` bound to your own thread, with `schedule` `{type: "interval", everyMs: <interval>}` and the tick prompt as `prompt`. The app's scheduler owns it rather than your session, so it should keep ticking through a session exit and a usage limit, and wake you on the first tick after the limit resets; F11's session-bound heartbeat died at both of its account-limit stops. Only the wake of an idle thread is probed (2026-10-11); confirm the rest the first time a run hits a limit, and record it in the decision trail. Every tick that lands during your turn is queued and delivered later, even after the task is deleted, so keep the interval longer than your longest turn and answer a stale tick with one line. Outside T3 Code, it is one background Bash `sleep <interval>` with `run_in_background`, re-armed as the last step of every tick, which dies with its session (`docs/work/reflect-kinds-2026-10-08/wake-probe.md`); for Codex outside T3 Code, see `../references/harness-tools.md`. An event to watch (a long command finishing, a merge, a ref advancing) gets a watcher subagent that wakes you on the event, with a long time-based heartbeat as fallback. No event gets a fixed-interval heartbeat sized to when the result is worth re-checking. After tick one, confirm its `decisions.tsv` row; only then report "the tick runs". When every open item waits on the operator, stop the heartbeat (`update_scheduled_task` with `enabled: false`) and re-arm it on their reply, because each idle tick re-reads your whole context. Delete it when the run ends.
3. Each iteration makes the smallest change the evidence justifies, verifies it against the predicate, commits if it advanced, discards changes that didn't help. Belt-and-suspenders that "might help" gets reverted, not left to ride.
   Sequence the work via the **sequence-verifiable-units** principle skill, verifying each unit before the next instead of batching checks at the end.
   When owners run units for you, brief each owner to rewrite `handoff.md` (branch, head, worktrees, open decisions, live helper ids, unfinished checks, next step) in the unit's scratch folder after every commit, merge, and helper start or stop, not only with reports, because a usage limit stops an owner between reports; F11's recovery found a whole phase missing from its handoff, and to save each census or audit result in the unit's facts folder, `~/.cache/agent-swarm/<unit>/facts/`. Open later briefs on a topic with "start from this" and that result's path. Cap the writers committing at once across the program at three per check slot (`checkSlots` in `scripts/feedback/check-slots.mjs`), split the cap across lane briefs, and have owners start their highest-value items first. Read-only reviewers don't count. Replace owners per the owner rule in `../../swarm/references/fix-rounds.md`, and retire their threads in the order in `../references/harness-tools.md` "Owner threads". Each tick runs `node scripts/swarm/stall-check.mjs <unit>` for every active unit and sends the owner of any unit that exits 1 the stalled worktree, since an owner idle on a silent helper never runs the check itself.
4. Mid-run discoveries are yours. Address broken skills, related bugs, flaky verifiers, review noise, tooling failures, orphaned follow-ups, and fixable drift yourself via poteto-mode. Put out-of-band fixes in their own commit. Do not park reversible work for the human or ask the user. Surface only irreversible actions, genuine product or preference calls no experiment can settle, or a real dead end. Keep the predicate as the main drive, and return to it after each side fix.
5. Checkpoint every iteration via the **show-me-your-work** skill, a row for what changed and whether the predicate moved.
6. Stop when the predicate is met. A plateau is not a stop, so keep going and pivot your approach to push past it. Surface a genuine dead end rather than spinning, and never relax the predicate to declare victory.

**Reply:** the exit condition, iterations run, what landed, what was discarded, final predicate state.
